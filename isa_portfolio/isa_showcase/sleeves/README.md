# Sleeves

A sleeve is an asset that is held under a certain market condition, with its holding being funded from the core. When the market condition is no longer true, that capital returns to the core, keeping full exposure. The 10+ individual sleeves are kept private, and only the combination of them all is shown.

Each sleeve began with an economic hypothesis as to why that asset should perform better under those certain market conditions. Since it is funded from the core, the sleeve must perform better than the core when it's held, have a positive Sharpe lower bound on what it adds to the book, and perform no worse than the core in its worst months.

## Results

- Combined contribution, 2006–2026: +3.6% a year relative to the core they replace, combined with 8% volatility.
- Combined, over the time period, correlation with the core was −0.35.
- With the sleeves, the book's Sharpe is higher in every era. In 2014–19 combined, the sleeves earned slightly less than an unusually strong core, but cut more risk than return, so the book's Sharpe still rose.

| era | Sharpe without sleeves | Sharpe with sleeves |
|---|---|---|
| 2006–13 | 0.81 | 1.31 |
| 2014–19 | 1.39 | 1.53 |
| 2020–26 | 0.84 | 1.73 |

## Before 2001

The same rules, unchanged and still combined, from February 1979 onwards. Index histories standing in for necessary ETFs, plain world equity standing in for the core. Start each era with £10,000, then, after modelled transaction costs:

| period | cash rate | CAGR without / with sleeves | Sharpe over cash without / with | return/vol without / with | max drawdown without / with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1979–1989 | 12.6% | 25.6% / 22.6% | 0.62 / 0.54 | 1.18 / 1.16 | −37.6% / −31.4% |
| 1990–2000 | 8.0% | 10.7% / 11.4% | 0.24 / 0.30 | 0.72 / 0.87 | −33.5% / −13.9% |
| 2001–2026 | 2.4% | 13.3% / 17.1% | 0.92 / 1.39 | 1.13 / 1.62 | −19.8% / −10.1% |

(Higher interest rates in earlier periods mean lower Sharpes. return/vol ignores the cash return to give fairer comparison)

In 1979-1989, the sleeves actually reduce the CAGR of the core. When investigated further, this was tied almost entirely to one asset, which had a terrible bear period here. Economically, it was correlated with the height of interest rates. Despite this, the Sharpe difference wasn't too large, and the sleeves still cut drawdown here. In the following decade, the pattern reversed, and the sleeves were beneficial to the book in all ways, Sharpe, CAGR, and cutting max drawdown by ~20%. The added Sharpe was not as large as it was in the rest of the time period, but it was still beneficial, despite the bear period continuing from the previous decade.

This tells us that the returns of the sleeves somewhat depend on the current market conditions experienced in the last 25 years, primarily low interest rates. Forwards in practice, the system needs some level of protection against the same regime returning, which is ongoing work.
