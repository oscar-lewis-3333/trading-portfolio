# Sleeves

A sleeve is an asset that is held under a certain market condition, with its holding being funded from the core. When the market condition is no longer true, that capital returns to the core, keeping full exposure. The 10+ individual sleeves are kept private, and only the combination of them all is shown.

Each sleeve began with an economic hypothesis as to why that asset should perform better under those certain market conditions. Since it is funded from the core, the sleeve must perform better than the core when its held, have a positive Sharpe lower bound on what it adds to the book, and perform no worse than the core in its worse months.

## Results

- Combined contribution, 2006–2026: +3.5% a year relative to the core they replace, combined with 8% volality.
- Combined, over the time period, correlation with the core was −0.37.
- With the sleeves, the book's Sharpe is higher in every era. In 2014–19 combined, the sleeves earned slightly less than an unusually strong core, but cut more risk than return, so the book's Sharpe still rose.

| era | Sharpe without sleeves | Sharpe with sleeves |
|---|---|---|
| 2006–13 | 0.80 | 1.34 |
| 2014–19 | 1.42 | 1.49 |
| 2020–26 | 0.91 | 2.00 |
