# Defensive layer

The core's worst performance is in recessions. This part of the book is designed to identify the start and ending of recessions, and what to hold in between the trigger sounding recession and re-entry. The trigger is built from general economic data, here is shown when it fired, and how long for.


## Trigger dates

| defensive from | to | months |
|---|---|---|
| Feb 2001 | Dec 2002 | 23 |
| May 2008 | May 2008 | 1 |
| Jul 2008 | Jul 2009 | 13 |
| Jul 2010 | Jul 2010 | 1 |
| Apr 2020 | Jun 2020 | 3 |

It caught the two primary, long recessions in 2001-2003, and Jul 2008-Jul 2009. It has one false re-entry in 2008, alongside a false alarm in 2010, and a late defensive exit and re-entry in 2020, due to the speed of the crash/recovery.

## Results

- Across the 41 defensive months, the core lost 8.4% a year (max drawdown −44.5%).
- Comparatively, defensive holdings made 5.4% a year (max drawdown −5.2%) across the same period.

February 2001 to September 2026, from £10,000, after modelled transaction costs:

| | CAGR | Sharpe | max drawdown | £10,000 becomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| core | 8.7% | 0.56 | −35.7% | £85,000 |
| core + defensive | 10.8% | 0.85 | −16.0% | £138,200 |

## Before 2001

The trigger was originally built in-sample on 2001-2026 using macro-economic data. When tested on an earlier time period, the following results were obtained:

| defensive from | to | months |
|---|---|---|
| May 1979 | May 1979 | 1 |
| Apr 1980 | Jun 1980 | 3 |
| Dec 1981 | Aug 1982 | 9 |
| Feb 1990 | Feb 1990 | 1 |
| Oct 1990 | Feb 1991 | 5 |
| Dec 1991 | Oct 1992 | 11 |
| Dec 2000 | Jan 2001 | 2 (continues above) |

February 1979 to January 2001, from £10,000, after modelled costs:

| | CAGR | Sharpe over cash | return / vol | max drawdown | £10,000 becomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| core (world equity) | 14.5% | 0.30 | 0.85 | −37.6% | £196,100 |
| core + defensive | 14.8% | 0.32 | 0.89 | −37.6% | £208,800 |

(Sharpe is measured over cash, with these interest rates averaging ~10.2%, so all Sharpe ratio's are massively lower, hence the 'return/vol' column)

The trigger found the early 1980s and 1990s recessions, assisting with both (In those months the defensive basket made 21.7% a year against 15.9% for equities). The worst fall in the period was not a recession, hence the trigger never fired, leading to its drawdown being unchanged. It is designed as a recession filter, not a crash filter
